How to Spot and Fix an Overworking Refrigerator Before Your Energy Bill Skyrockets

Long refrigerator run time is not always a fault. Compare temperature, door sealing, airflow and recent changes before deciding on repair.

Book service onlineMore repair guides

A refrigerator that runs for long periods is not automatically broken. Operating time varies with the model, room conditions, door opening and recent loading. The useful question is whether it maintains the intended temperature and whether its behavior has changed. Do not replace a thermostat or compressor based on run time alone.

When longer operation can be normal

Room heat, frequent door opening and adding food can increase cooling demand. GE Appliances explains that normal refrigerator cycle times can include long periods of operation. Check the guidance for your own model instead of treating a fixed on/off interval as a universal standard.

Measure the condition you are concerned about

Record refrigerator and freezer temperatures using an appliance thermometer and the placement guidance in your manual. Note the control setting, when the reading was taken and whether doors were recently opened. A control display may show a target rather than the temperature at every location. If cooling is unreliable, protect temperature-sensitive food and follow food-safety guidance.

  • Is one compartment warm while the other remains cold?
  • Has frost appeared on an interior panel?
  • Is a door being held open by a shelf, drawer or package?
  • Has the appliance recently been moved or loaded?
  • Are there new sounds, leaks or repeated temperature changes?

Checks that do not require dismantling

Keep food packages clear of accessible internal vents and make sure doors close without obstruction. Inspect visible seals for dirt or damage and clean them according to the manual. A weak seal test alone does not prove that the gasket needs replacement; alignment and obstructions also need consideration.

Check the manufacturer's maintenance instructions before attempting condenser cleaning. Access and cleaning requirements vary. Do not remove covers, bend tubing or move a heavy appliance to reach components. Disconnect power as required by the manual for permitted maintenance and leave inaccessible components to a technician.

When professional diagnosis is useful

Arrange service if the appliance cannot maintain temperature, repeatedly frosts up, leaks or has a persistent change in behavior that ordinary user checks do not explain. Diagnosis may involve airflow, fans, defrost operation, sensors, controls, door sealing or the sealed system. Several faults can cause similar symptoms.

A higher household electricity bill by itself does not establish the refrigerator as the cause. Compare actual appliance behavior and relevant usage information before paying for a repair. Ask for an explained diagnosis and estimate rather than a guaranteed energy-saving claim.

Repair or replace?

Compare the specific repair with the appliance's condition, parts support and the total cost of an appropriate replacement, including installation. Age or a single cost percentage should not decide every case. If warranty coverage may apply, check its service requirements first.
