KitchenAid Double Oven Not Heating in Carmel, Indiana

The lower oven in a Carmel homeowner’s KitchenAid gas range would not heat, and the upper oven heated poorly. Alex Appliance Repair diagnosed the KFGD500ESS01, replaced three oven igniters, reassembled the range and tested both ovens.

The Service Call for Two Different Heating Symptoms

A Carmel homeowner called Alex Appliance Repair about two problems on the same KitchenAid gas range. The lower oven would not heat at all, and the upper oven heated poorly. Both symptoms mattered because a double oven has separate burner functions to evaluate.

Our technician diagnosed the range on site and determined that all three oven igniters needed replacement. The repair covered the lower bake burner, the upper bake burner and the upper broil burner. After installing the new igniters, we reassembled the range and checked operation.

Identifying the KitchenAid Double Oven

The appliance was a stainless steel KitchenAid double-oven gas range. The first photos show the two oven doors and the separate control groups on the back panel. Those controls gave us a clear way to talk about which cavity and mode were affected when the customer described the heating problem.

The full model on the rating plate is KFGD500ESS01. The final 01 identifies the version serviced. A clear photo of the model field is especially useful when a range has more than one oven and uses different igniter configurations.

Why the Upper and Lower Ovens Were Checked Separately

On this KitchenAid, the upper cavity has separate bake and broil burners, and the lower cavity has its own bake burner. The model label identifies all three functions. A single range can therefore show several heating symptoms at once even though the controls and oven lights still come on.

A hot-surface igniter gets hot enough to light gas at its burner. A glow can be visible before flame appears, so a picture of a glowing igniter and a picture of a lit burner document different moments in the ignition sequence.

KitchenAid’s model overview shows the two-cavity range. KitchenAid model overview

Accessing the Ignition Components

The rear service photos show the wiring area with the back cover off. The oven-cavity photos show the other side of the job, where the bake burner and its igniter sit beneath the oven floor. Seeing both areas helps explain why servicing a covered bake burner involves access beyond the control panel.

In the lower oven, the bake burner runs along the floor of the cavity. Its igniter is mounted near the back end of the burner, where the close-up shows the vented metal housing beside the burner tube.

Replacing Three Oven Igniters

We replaced the three igniters identified during diagnosis. The close-ups show a removed burner section with its bracket and braided leads, and an igniter housing beside a bake burner tube. The supplied diagram in the visit overview locates the igniter styles relative to the burner assemblies.

The two reference images show differences in lead length and mounting shape. Position and fit matter on a double-oven range because each igniter has to sit next to its own burner and reach the corresponding connection.

Checking the Upper Oven Bake Burner

The upper bake check is shown in the wider photo of the open cavity and in the side view of the burner area. Blue flame is visible beneath the burner shield. The other close-up shows the igniter glowing near the burner during the ignition sequence.

These views document the bake burner at the shield and at the igniter itself. The broil burner sits overhead and was checked separately.

Checking the Upper Oven Broil Burner

These frames show the overhead broil burner as the igniter glows and as blue flame becomes visible along the burner ports. The wider view places the broil burner at the top of the upper oven, above the floor shield that covers the bake burner.

The next two photographs show the lit burner from different angles, including the area immediately beside the igniter. That distinction is useful when an upper oven heats poorly in one mode but behaves differently in another.

Testing Continued Through Reassembly

After the igniters were installed, the technician reassembled the KitchenAid range, turned it on and tested both ovens. The upper bake and upper broil checks addressed the complaint about weak heating in the smaller cavity. The lower bake check addressed the cavity that had stayed cold.

The service photographs of the exposed burners document ignition during the inspection. The range was checked again after reassembly.

Lower Oven Heat After the Igniter Replacement

The lower oven’s bake burner lit during testing. The close-ups show blue flame along the tube and the igniter glowing nearby, with the convection fan cover visible in the wider frame.

The final service view shows both cavities and the control panel during the check. The repair visit ended with both ovens tested after reassembly.

Questions About KitchenAid Double Oven Heating

Can the upper oven heat poorly while the lower one stays cold?

Yes. Each cavity has its own bake burner, and this model also has an upper broil burner. On this range, diagnosis found that all three oven igniters needed replacement. The same symptoms on another range should be checked before parts are ordered.

Does a glowing igniter mean the burner has lit?

A glowing igniter is one stage of the sequence. The burner lighting is a separate observation. The service photos in this case show both the orange glow of the igniter and blue flame at the burners.

What should I mention when booking a visit?

Share the full model number, which oven is affected, and whether the problem happens in Bake, Broil or both. It also helps to describe whether the oven stays cold or starts heating but struggles during preheat.
