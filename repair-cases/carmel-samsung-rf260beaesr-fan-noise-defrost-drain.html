The Complaint: Fan Noise That Stopped When the Doors Opened

A homeowner in Carmel, Indiana contacted Alex Appliance Repair because their Samsung refrigerator was making a fan noise. The distinctive detail was that the noise disappeared when the refrigerator doors were opened. We visited the home to diagnose the appliance rather than selecting a replacement part from the symptom alone.

The identification label confirmed model Samsung RF260BEAESR/AA. This repair focused on the fresh-food compartment: the refrigerator section above the freezer drawer. The photographs below document the actual service visit, with a separate component reference illustration in the overview.

Model Confirmed: RF260BEAESR/AA

This close-up from the refrigerator identification sticker records model RF260BEAESR and the full code RF260BEAESR/AA used for this service visit. Checking the appliance label helps identify the correct configuration when selecting replacement components.

Inspecting Behind the Fresh-Food Rear Cover

We removed the shelves and opened the rear interior cover to inspect the evaporator area. Diagnosis revealed ice buildup at the evaporator coil and in the drain line. The upper tubing area also carried a substantial accumulation of ice, visible in the close-up photographs.

The customer's description helped focus the inspection on the fresh-food fan area, but the repair needed to address more than the noise alone. The evaporator, drainage path, temperature sensor and fan were all part of the work completed during this visit.

Evaporator Ice: What We Found

With the cover removed, we could see frost on the coil and a large block of ice above it. These photographs show the condition encountered during diagnosis. We thawed the evaporator and removed all accumulated ice before completing the repair. That gave us access to the affected drain area and the components being serviced.

Clearing the Drain From Inside and Behind the Refrigerator

We cleaned the drain line from inside the fresh-food compartment, then accessed the lower rear compartment. The rear drain tubes were removed and cleaned from that side as well. This addressed the drainage path from both access points, not just the visible ice inside the cabinet. The photographs show the rear service opening and the drain tubes beside the condenser; the tubes are pictured in their installed positions.

Adding a Flexible Heater Alongside the Existing Defrost Heater

An additional flexible heating element was installed as part of this repair. The installation photograph shows its position along the upper tubing during service; frost is still visible at that stage of the work. The second image shows the existing defrost heating element below the coil. The new flexible heater was an addition, not a replacement for that original heating element. Its operation was checked during the forced-defrost test described below.

Replacing the Temperature Sensor

We replaced the temperature sensor as part of the fresh-food evaporator service. The work-surface photograph shows several sensor components available during the visit; one temperature sensor was replaced in this refrigerator.

The overview illustration helps locate the fan and sensor relative to the interior cover. It is a supplied reference image, while the repair photographs show the actual appliance. Part selection for another Samsung refrigerator requires checking its exact model and configuration.

Replacing the Fresh-Food Evaporator Fan

The fresh-food evaporator fan was replaced with a new unit. In the comparison photograph, the original fan is still in the removed cover and the replacement is beside it. The next image shows the new fan mounted during the repair. This was the fan serving the refrigerator compartment, not the condenser fan in the lower rear machinery compartment.

Forced-Defrost Test: Both Heaters Operating Together

After the component work, we checked the system in test mode and activated a forced defrost cycle. This confirmed that the additional flexible heater operated at the same time as the existing defrost heating element. That operating check was an important part of verifying the installation.

The completed work included thawing the ice, cleaning the drain from the interior and rear, replacing the temperature sensor and evaporator fan, and installing and testing the additional heater. The photograph records the service setup; it is not a display of a measured temperature or a test-mode reading.
