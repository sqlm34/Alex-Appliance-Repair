Problems we diagnose

Common microwave symptoms

A visible symptom identifies the place to begin. Model-specific testing determines whether the cause is a component, installation condition or utility connection.

Runs but does not heat

High-voltage components, controls and power delivery require trained testing because stored voltage can be dangerous.

Door will not latch

Hooks, switches, mounts and alignment must operate in the correct sequence for safe startup.

Turntable does not move

The motor, coupler, tray support and control output are checked after confirming the tray is seated.

Display or keypad is unresponsive

Power supply, membrane controls, user-interface boards and main controls can cause similar symptoms.

Vent fan or light fails

Motors, bulbs, sockets, relays and control outputs are evaluated on over-the-range models.

Sparking inside the cavity

Metal contamination, damaged waveguide covers, racks or cavity damage must be identified before reuse.

Detailed service guide

Modern life has made us dependent on microwaves in ways previous generations never anticipated. Heating leftover coffee, warming a quick snack, defrosting dinner, and popping popcorn are tiny tasks that happen throughout the day.

Then your microwave stops working and suddenly you are without quick heating solutions. The good news is that most microwave problems are fixable. You probably do not need a new microwave. You need someone to figure out what is actually wrong.

Why Microwaves Fail

Microwaves contain several high-voltage and electronic components. When one fails, the symptoms can look similar from the outside, so proper diagnosis matters.

  • The magnetron has given up. This component generates the microwave energy that heats food. When it fails, the microwave may run, the plate may rotate, and the light may come on, but nothing gets warm.
  • The high voltage transformer is not working. This part supplies the power the magnetron needs. Without it, the microwave cannot heat correctly.
  • The capacitor or diode has failed. These parts help create the high voltage needed for heating. If either fails, the microwave can turn on but stop heating food.
  • The control board is having issues. A failing control board can cause buttons not to respond, random behavior, or complete power failure.
  • The door interlock switch thinks the door is open. This safety switch prevents operation when the door is open. If it fails, the microwave may not run even when the door is closed.

What's Actually Worth Fixing

A microwave less than 10 years old that needs a magnetron, switch, fan, diode, or control component may be worth repairing. A microwave that is 15 years old or older with a major control failure may be better to replace. We will tell you honestly what makes sense for your situation.

Diagnosing the Problem

When you call, the symptom usually tells us where to start. If the microwave turns on but does not heat, we check heating components. If it will not turn on at all, we check power, the control board, fuses, and door switches. If it heats but makes unusual noises, we inspect the magnetron, cooling fan, and moving parts.

Microwave Brands We Work On

We service LG, Samsung, GE, Whirlpool, Electrolux, Maytag, Panasonic, Toshiba, Emerson, and other microwave brands. Over-the-range microwaves and countertop models are different in installation and access, but the core heating components work in similar ways.

Real Repair Scenarios

We have repaired microwaves that ran but stopped heating because the magnetron failed. We have fixed unusual whining noises caused by a failing cooling fan. We have replaced bad door seals and control boards that made over-the-range units appear completely dead. In many cases, repair costs much less than replacing the whole appliance.

Why Your Microwave Matters More Than You Think

Microwaves are efficiency machines. They make quick meals, quick reheats, and quick solutions part of the day. When they stop working, you feel it immediately in your routine.

Simple Troubleshooting Before You Call

  • Check that the microwave is plugged in.
  • Check whether a breaker has tripped.
  • Try another outlet if it is a countertop unit and you can do so safely.
  • If it runs but does not heat, try a normal-length cycle to confirm the issue.

Microwave Safety

Do not try to replace a magnetron, capacitor, transformer, or high-voltage diode yourself. Microwave components can hold dangerous voltage. If the microwave smells like burning, sparks, makes unusual noises, or does not seem right, stop using it and call for service.

Cost of Replacement vs Repair

A basic new microwave may be inexpensive, but your current microwave may already fit your space, match your kitchen, and have controls you know. Over-the-range replacement can also involve installation work. Repair is often the simpler and more affordable path when the appliance is otherwise in good condition.

Frequently asked questions

Microwave Repair questions

Is a microwave worth repairing?

Built-in and over-the-range units are often practical to repair when the cabinet, cavity and installation are in good condition.

Why does the microwave run without heating?

Several high-voltage or control faults can cause this symptom. Safe electrical testing is required.

Can a broken door switch stop the microwave?

Yes. Multiple interlock switches monitor the door, and a failed or misaligned switch can prevent operation.

Do you repair countertop microwaves?

Service availability depends on model, parts and repair economics. Built-in and over-the-range units are the primary focus.
