Dryer Repair

Signs Your Dryer Needs Repairs Before It Breaks Down

Distinguish changes in drying time, heat, noise and drum movement, with safe preparation steps before a repair visit.

Book service onlineMore repair guides

A change in how your dryer operates is more useful than a fixed rule about how many minutes a cycle should take. Compare similar loads and settings. Note whether the problem is weak heat, damp clothing, a new noise, a stopped drum or failure to start. Different symptoms lead to different checks.

Clothes need another cycle

A clogged lint screen or restricted exhaust can lengthen drying time, but the load, selected cycle, heating output and moisture sensing also matter. Clean the lint screen as directed in the manual and check that the selected cycle uses heat. Do not assume that long dry times prove the heating element has failed.

For a vented dryer, report any visible crushed transition duct or change after the appliance was moved. Do not disconnect the exhaust and run the dryer indoors. Building exhaust cleaning and appliance repair are different tasks; inaccessible wall or roof ducting may need a dedicated vent-cleaning provider. Whirlpool's long drying time guidance explains the role of lint and restricted airflow.

New squealing, rumbling or thumping

Record when the sound occurs and whether the drum turns. With the appliance stopped, check for loose objects in the accessible drum. Do not remove covers to inspect moving components. Belt, roller, idler, motor and blower conditions can produce overlapping symptoms and need to be distinguished during diagnosis.

Our LG dryer tensioner and roller example shows cabinet access and documented component work. It illustrates a repair process rather than proving that another noisy dryer needs the same parts.

Burning smell, excessive heat or repeated stopping

Stop using the dryer if there is a burning smell, scorching or abnormal heat. Do not keep restarting it, bypass a safety device or repeatedly reset a tripping breaker. Have the appliance and relevant airflow or electrical conditions evaluated before further use. If gas is suspected, leave the area and contact the gas utility or emergency service from a safe location.

The drum does not turn or the dryer has no heat

Tell the provider whether the controls respond, whether a motor sound is heard and whether the drum moves during the fault. Identify gas or electric equipment if known. Do not reach inside a running dryer or open the cabinet to check a belt. A repair recommendation should follow model-specific checks rather than replacement by guesswork.

What to prepare for the technician

  • Brand and model from an accessible label.
  • Gas, electric or heat-pump equipment if known.
  • The cycle, load and point at which the problem occurs.
  • Any error code or a recording of a noise already observed.
  • Stacked installation, tight closet or difficult access.
